\begin{abstract}
A change of rotation system can change which closed walks represent homology without changing the underlying adjacency. We construct integral periods for finite signed face walks, verify a genus-two counterexample, and compare two encodings of the same four observed FX values. In a reused October split, planar phase features gave the lowest mean absolute error among four ridge fits at every evaluated horizon. The one-second advantage over the genus-two phase features was about $2.9\times10^{-5}$ log basis points.
\end{abstract}

\section{Integral coordinates}
Let $E$ be the stable oriented edges of a connected cellular map on a closed orientable surface. A face is a word of signed edge occurrences. Each directed dart is used once. Preserve both occurrences when the same face traverses an edge twice. Let $\partial_1$ be the vertex boundary and $\partial_2$ the signed face boundary.

\textbf{Construction.} Choose a primal spanning tree $T$. From the dual edges outside $T$, choose a dual spanning tree $C$. For each leftover edge $h$ let $c_h$ traverse $h$ positively and return along $T$. These $2g$ cycles give a basis for $H_1$ over $\mathbb Z$.

\textbf{Proof.} The dual edges outside $T$ are connected. Otherwise the boundary of a nonempty proper union of dual components would be a nonzero integral cycle supported on the primal tree. This is impossible. The dual of a connected closed surface cellulation is connected, so that union has a boundary edge. For a closed integral edge chain $z$, solve $(\partial_2 x)_e=z_e$ for $e\in C$. Fix one face potential to zero and propagate along $C$. Every dual tree equation is the difference of two face potentials with unit coefficients, so the solution is integral. The chain $z-\partial_2x$ vanishes on $C$. Subtract its coefficients on the leftover edges times their corresponding $c_h$. The remaining chain is closed and supported on $T$, hence zero. The same argument with $z$ a boundary proves uniqueness, since a face potential constant across $C$ is constant everywhere. The count is $|E|-(|V|-1)-(|F|-1)=2g$.

For an Abelian connection $a$, changing $a$ by a vertex coboundary leaves the evaluation of every closed walk unchanged. Replacing a walk by $c'=\sum_h M_h c_h + \partial_2 x$ gives
\[
 \langle a,c'\rangle = \sum_h M_h\langle a,c_h\rangle
       + \langle \partial_2^{\mathsf T}a,x\rangle .
\]
The second term disappears for flat $a$, including circle-valued transport flat modulo $2\pi$.

\section{Fixed-adjacency witness}
Matching rotations on $K_{2,5}$ give one face and genus two. Reversing one rotation gives five faces and genus zero. Give the first edge exponent $1$ in $\mathbb Z/3$ and the others exponent $0$. The first embedding gives zero face circulation and period exponents $(2,2,2,2)$. The planar embedding gives two opposite nonzero face circulations and no free periods. The corresponding $K_{2,9}$ comparison has genus four versus genus zero. These are exact finite identities.

\section{Recorded observations}
Each window contains sixteen measured one-second FX frames. At relative offsets $-15,-11,-7,0$, take half the forward-minus-reverse sum of the six directional log quotes around the triangle. Put those four values on four of the length-two ribbon paths. The reference path and all first legs carry structural zero. Genus-two periods recover the input vector, while four independent planar face circulations are related by an integer matrix of determinant $\pm 1$.

Both periodic fits add the sine and shifted cosine of each resulting coordinate at $0.5$ radians per log bp. The tanh control adds $\tanh(x)$ and $\tanh^2(x)$ at the same scale of the genus-two period vector. All fits share the raw graph features and ridge penalty $30$. A missing direction at any of the four selected steps masks the extra channels. The fit uses $262{,}144$ training windows, of which $38{,}011$ have complete four-frame support. The October split has $65{,}536$ anchor windows and $12{,}513$ complete phase inputs. Target masks vary by horizon, with identical target keys across arms.
